Making it look like one rifle

  • Keep the hardware black. Every scope, ring set and can here ships in black or near-black, which sits cleanly against a green and tan stock and a dark carbon barrel.
  • Use the suppressor cover as the color accent. Coyote or ranger green picks up the stock. It also cuts mirage and keeps a hot titanium can off your pack and hands.
  • Pick one tan and stay in it. Burnt bronze, FDE and coyote are three different colors and rarely match side by side. A coyote cover and sling next to a bronze action looks deliberate; an FDE can next to it usually does not.
  • The odd ones out: the Nomad Ti XC ships in sniper grey and the Scythe-Ti in a dark oil-rubbed anodize. Both look fine under a cover and mismatched without one.
  • Avoid silver. Bright stainless ring screws, a bare level or a silver bipod will be the first thing the eye lands on.

How to read the numbers

  • Weights, prices and PEW ratings are published figures gathered October 7, 2026. A tilde marks an estimate I could not verify.
  • Criteria marked my rating are my 1 to 10 judgment, because no published number exists for glass quality, toughness or stability. Change the slider to 0 to remove one from the score.
  • Scores are relative within each list: 100 means best in the list on every criterion you weighted.
  • PEW Science tested every can on a 20 in .308. Your 16 in barrel will be louder with all of them; the order should hold.
  • No ammunition maker publishes a minimum expansion velocity for these loads. The floors used here are commonly relayed figures and are labeled as assumed.
